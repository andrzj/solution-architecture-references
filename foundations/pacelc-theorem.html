PACELC Theorem

A refinement of CAP that accounts for latency trade-offs even in the absence of partitions — the realistic model for database selection.

⏱ 6 min read

What it is

PACELC extends the CAP theorem. When a Partition happens, you choose Availability or Consistency. Else — on a healthy network — you choose low Latency or Consistency on every replicated write.

A ride-hailing platform often runs two stores side by side. Driver GPS pings land in DynamoDB with default eventual consistency (PA/EL): a map pin may lag briefly, but drivers keep streaming updates without waiting on far replicas. Driver payout balances sit in a strongly consistent ledger (PC/EC): a partition may block some writes, but two nodes never disagree about money owed.

CAP alone labels both Cassandra-class and DynamoDB-class systems “AP.” PACELC explains why you still need two different designs — partition behavior and day-to-day latency behavior are separate questions.

Why it exists

Most outages that users notice happen while the network is fine. CAP only speaks up when links fail; PACELC names the trade-off engineers feel every sprint: acknowledge a write after one local replica (EL) or wait for a quorum (EC).

Daniel Abadi’s PACELC formulation (2010) also separates databases that look identical on a CAP slide. DynamoDB and Cassandra both tolerate partitions, but their default write paths favor low latency. Designs in the PNUTS family stay available under partition yet pursue stronger consistency when connected — a different PACELC posture teams document before picking either for payouts.

When to use

  • Use PACELC when your team is down to two or more distributed databases and stakeholders care about both freshness and response time.
  • Use it when the product mixes hot paths — live driver maps — with cold correctness paths — payout settlement — and you need separate consistency postures for each.
  • Use it when you deploy across regions, because quorum writes usually pay a cross-region round trip that EL backends avoid.
  • Use it when finance, compliance, or safety reviewers ask why the architecture accepts stale reads anywhere, and you need a named framework rather than ad hoc excuses.

When not to use

  • Do not treat PACELC as a substitute for reading the vendor’s consistency documentation; tunable levels blur the EL/EC label on every operation.
  • Do not apply CAP or PACELC to a single-node database; partition tolerance only matters once independent failure domains exist.
  • Do not stamp a database PA/EL forever in an ADR without retesting after upgrades, new consistency defaults, or multi-region layout changes.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Teams can explain why GPS and payouts use different databases without rehashing CAP lectures every quarter.
  • Engineers surface the latency tax of quorum writes before production traffic proves the API is too slow.
  • Product and finance stakeholders hear a shared vocabulary — PA/EL pins versus ledger rows — during roadmap trade-off meetings.
  • Architecture reviews compare candidates on both failure behavior and happy-path write latency, not logo slides.

Cons

  • Labels collapse when Cassandra consistency levels differ per query; you must document operation-level settings, not one sticker per cluster.
  • Executives rarely know PACELC; staff must translate into user-visible outcomes (stale map vs delayed payout).
  • Durability, query models, licensing, and ops headcount still decide the shortlist — PACELC does not replace load tests.
  • A mistaken PA/EL choice on payouts is expensive to unwind once finance workflows depend on the store.

Implementation notes

Split stores by PACELC posture: Keep driver locations on DynamoDB global tables with default eventual consistency. Payout accruals run through a regional Postgres cluster with synchronous replication to a standby — PC/EC behavior documented in an ADR.

Tune within one engine: On Cassandra, consistency level ONE is EL: the coordinator acks after the first replica. QUORUM shifts toward EC on the same cluster. Inventory reservations during surge pricing might use QUORUM while session tokens stay at ONE.

Selection workflow: For each datastore, ask whether stale reads are acceptable (rules out naive EL for balances). Define partition behavior for rider safety and payouts (can the map go dark?). Benchmark quorum write latency at expected peak GPS rate. Record the chosen PACELC posture and per-operation levels in an ADR before cutover.

Common failure modes

  • One database for everything: Payout accruals live in the same PA/EL table as GPS pings, then finance finds duplicate adjustments after concurrent writes.
  • Quorum on the hot path: The rider app loads nearby drivers through synchronous multi-region Postgres; p99 latency jumps and riders abandon the session.
  • Assumed labels: The team markets the stack as “strongly consistent” without chaos tests; the first AZ failure shows writes failing open in unexpected ways.
  • Ignored partition plan: A PC ledger has no runbook for minority-side behavior, so on-call engineers guess during a real network split.

Decision checklist

  • Can this read path tolerate briefly stale data without violating safety or money rules?
  • Have we measured write latency at peak load for both EL and EC settings on the finalists?
  • Will the deployment span multiple regions or availability zones that add replication round trips?
  • During a partition, must riders still see a map, must payouts still accrue, or may we fail closed?
  • Have we run partition injection tests on the exact topology and consistency levels we plan to ship?
  • For each API operation, is the configured consistency level documented and reviewed with the owning team?
  • Does an ADR state the accepted PACELC posture and link to benchmarks that justified it?

Example use cases

  • Illustrative scenario — RideRelay driver map (PA/EL): GPS pings write to DynamoDB; riders see slightly stale pins but the dispatch service never blocks on cross-region quorum.
  • Illustrative scenario — RideRelay weekly payouts (PC/EC): Accrual rows live in a consensus-backed ledger; a partition may reject writes, but duplicate payout lines do not appear.
  • Illustrative scenario — RideRelay surge inventory (PA + tunable EC): Promotional ride credits use EL counters; limited “surge bonus” slots use QUORUM decrements so two regions cannot oversell the same pool.
