Batch Processing

Offline computation over bounded datasets—ETL, ML feature builds, and periodic reconciliations at warehouse scale.

⏱ 10 min read

What it is

Why it exists

Many business questions do not require second-level freshness: financial close, search index rebuilds, training snapshots, and compliance exports. Batch provides strong correctness (rerun the same job) and economical use of spot/preemptible compute.

When to use

  • Daily or hourly ETL/ELT into a lakehouse or warehouse.
  • Model training snapshots and feature backfills.
  • Large-scale reconciliation and auditing.
  • Index or cache warmers fed from warehouse exports.

When not to use

  • Fraud detection or live dashboards needing sub-minute latency.
  • Per-event regulatory reporting without micro-batch compromise.

Typical architecture

Scheduler triggers idempotent jobs; raw zones are append-only.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Cost-efficient at scale; easy replay and audit.
  • Simpler correctness than distributed streaming.

Cons

  • Latency bounded by schedule.
  • Peak resource usage; needs capacity planning.

Implementation notes

Partition inputs by date or business key; make jobs idempotent with deterministic outputs. Use orchestration sensors for data readiness, not wall-clock alone. Store lineage and row counts; alert on anomaly thresholds. Prefer open table formats (Parquet, Iceberg, Delta) for ACID merges.

Common failure modes

  • Silent partial writes: Jobs succeed but write incomplete partitions.
  • Non-idempotent reruns: Duplicated facts after retry.

Decision checklist

  • Can the job be safely rerun for the same partition?
  • Are inputs immutable and outputs versioned?
  • Is SLA defined as data freshness, not wall time?

Example use cases

  • Nightly sales mart: Spark aggregates POS and e-commerce into a gold table for executive dashboards.
