Data Pipeline Patterns

Medallion architecture (Bronze/Silver/Gold), idempotent pipelines, incremental processing with watermarks and checkpointing, orchestration tools (Airflow, Prefect, Dagster), data quality with Great Expectations, and lineage tracking.

⏱ 12 min read

What it is

Data pipeline patterns are reusable design choices for moving and refining data at scale: layered lakehouse layouts (medallion bronze, silver, gold), idempotent writes, incremental processing with checkpoints, orchestration, and quality validation between stages. They turn fragile one-off ETL into repeatable mechanics that survive retries, schema change, and operational scrutiny.

Patterns compose—raw append-only landing, contracted cleaning, business aggregates, expectation suites on promotion—not as dogma but as defaults teams can adopt when reliability matters more than the shortest path to a single report.

Why it exists

Pipelines fail in predictable ways: APIs return partial pages, clusters restart mid-partition, upstream columns appear overnight, and someone reruns yesterday’s job without clearing duplicates. Without shared patterns, each team reinvents watermarks, merge semantics, and quarantine flows—and still ships subtle data corruption.

Documented patterns (medallion layering, partition overwrite, merge upserts, orchestrator sensors, Great Expectations gates) give reviewers a checklist and give operators known recovery steps when freshness or quality SLAs break.

When to use

  • You operate lakehouse or warehouse pipelines with multiple sources and downstream consumers depending on consistent grains.
  • Teams need standard answers for idempotency, incremental loads, backfills, and late-arriving events.
  • Compliance or product SLAs require provable lineage and blocking quality gates between layers.
  • Batch and streaming jobs must honor the same schema contracts and freshness definitions.

When not to use

  • A throwaway script with one author and no recurring schedule does not justify medallion overhead.
  • A managed sync product owns extract, load, and basic typing and your team only models in the warehouse.
  • Data volume and consumer count are so small that a single table plus cron satisfies every requirement.

Typical architecture

Medallion layering, idempotent transforms, incremental checkpoints, and orchestrated quality checks chain together from source ingest through business-ready serving tables.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Immutable bronze retention lets teams fix transformation bugs by replaying from raw history without re-extracting sources.
  • Idempotent partition overwrite or merge semantics make retries safe after transient cluster or network failures.
  • Incremental watermarks and change feeds cut daily compute to new or changed rows instead of full-history scans.
  • Quality suites at silver boundaries stop corrupt batches before they reach metrics consumers.

Cons

  • Three logical layers multiply storage unless retention and compaction policies are enforced.
  • Orchestrator clusters (Airflow, Dagster, Prefect) add another system to patch, monitor, and capacity-plan.
  • Checkpoint corruption or clock-skewed watermarks create duplicate or missing slices until manually repaired.
  • Expectation libraries require maintenance whenever schemas or business rules evolve.

Implementation notes

Schema evolution: Allow additive changes at bronze ingestion; enforce contracted types and names at silver. Plan explicit migration jobs for breaking changes instead of silent casts downstream.

Idempotency: Prefer partition-scoped overwrite or MERGE keyed on business identifiers; avoid blind append on retry.

Late-arriving data: Either overlap processing windows with idempotent overwrites or maintain a grace interval in the watermark; document the maximum lateness the pipeline guarantees.

Orchestration choice: DAG-centric tools fit stable schedules; asset-centric tools (Dagster) shine when lineage and data-aware scheduling are first-class requirements.

Common failure modes

  • Append-only retries: Re-running a failed job that used INSERT duplicates facts in silver or gold.
  • Skipped quality gate: Invalid bronze batches propagate to executive metrics because checks only log warnings.
  • Stale checkpoints: A manually edited or restored checkpoint replays the wrong window and double-counts events.

Decision checklist

  • Is bronze append-only with raw payloads preserved for replay?
  • Do silver and gold writes use idempotent overwrite or merge semantics?
  • Is the incremental watermark or change-feed cursor stored durably and recoverable?
  • Do blocking quality expectations run before data promotes to downstream layers?
  • Do quality failures page an owner instead of only writing to logs?

Example use cases

  • Illustrative scenario — An e-commerce team runs a nightly orchestrated DAG that lands orders in bronze object storage, validates with expectation suites, merges into silver Delta tables, and publishes gold revenue aggregates by region.
  • Illustrative scenario — A product analytics team continuously writes Kafka events to bronze, micro-batches silver deduplication and enrichment, and publishes hourly gold rollups for self-serve dashboards.
