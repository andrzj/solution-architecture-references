Security Architecture

Authorization Models

RBAC, ABAC, ReBAC, and policy-based authorization — choosing the right access control model.

⏱ 10 min read

What it is

Authorization Models is the pattern to use when multi-team products with differing permission needs. Authorization models define how a system decides whether an authenticated principal may perform an action on a resource. Common models include RBAC (roles), ABAC (attributes and policies), ReBAC (relationship graphs), and policy engines (OPA, Cedar).

Example: a nurse role may read patient vitals in their ward; ABAC adds attributes such as shift time and care assignment; ReBAC expresses nurse:alice is caregiver for patient:bob.

Why it exists

Authentication alone is insufficient — every breach story includes over-privileged accounts. Explicit authorization models make least privilege enforceable, auditable, and testable across microservices.

When to use

  • Multi-team products with differing permission needs.
  • Regulated data requiring attribute or purpose-based access.
  • Microservices needing consistent policy enforcement.
  • Partner APIs with fine-grained scopes.

When not to use

  • Single-admin internal tools with one role — keep RBAC minimal.
  • Mixing models without a policy source of truth — leads to drift.

Typical architecture

Policy decision point (PDP) centralizes authorization.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • RBAC is simple to explain and audit.
  • ABAC supports context-aware least privilege.
  • Central PDP prevents divergent ad-hoc checks.
  • Policy-as-code enables versioned reviews.

Cons

  • ABAC policies can become opaque without tooling.
  • Central PDP adds latency unless cached.
  • Role explosion in large RBAC matrices.
  • ReBAC graphs require operational maturity.

Implementation notes

Enforce authorization in the service layer, not only at the gateway. Represent permissions as explicit statements (resource, action, scope) and default deny.

Store policies in Git; test with table-driven policy unit tests. Log allow/deny with principal, resource, and policy version.

Common failure modes

  • Coarse roles: everyone is admin in staging and prod.
  • Missing object scope: role allows read:order without order-id check (BOLA).
  • Stale cache: denied user still allowed until TTL expires.
  • Dual models: RBAC in app, ABAC in gateway — inconsistent outcomes.

Decision checklist

  • Is there a single policy source of truth?
  • Are decisions default-deny?
  • Are object-level checks mandatory?
  • Are policies tested in CI?
  • Are denials logged for security monitoring?
  • Is emergency break-glass separate and audited?

Example use cases

  • Healthcare: ABAC on patient records using assignment + treatment purpose.
  • SaaS: org-scoped RBAC with custom roles per tenant.
  • Google Zanzibar-style: ReBAC for document sharing graphs.
